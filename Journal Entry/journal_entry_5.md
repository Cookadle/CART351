#Journal Entry 5

Our household is getting a 3D printer. How fun ~ and I am very excited to try it! Continuing on the topic of finding a medium or a niche per say, I am eager to get my hands into 3D printing. 

I love fashion, and I love collecting intricate and unique pieces of jewelry. I find pleasure in curating my wardrobe and reinventing my clothes to fit me and how I feel. Naturally, combining something I enjoy with my field of study can be quite difficult, but having a 3D printer at home might finally give me the freedom to just let loose and experiment.

I have been inspired before by Ashley Zelinskie's work [Ashley Zelinskie Portfolio](https://ashleyzelinskie.com). A couple of years ago, she created a series called *Reverse Abstraction*, which was featured in this [Make: Magazine Article](https://makezine.com/article/craft/reverse-abstraction/). In her own words, this series:
> "...begins with a simple premise: that humans and computers perceive the world through different languages, and what is concrete for one is abstract for the other."

Her motto is that she creates "art for the future."Which could be the reason why I still remember her work 10 years after finding out about it back in 2018.

I have many jewelry heirlooms passed down from my family members. With the way so many resources are now starting to be scarce these days, it is obvious that certain materials will eventually be considered rare or tabboooo (such as fur or leather). Even though the long-term durability of a 3D-printed object is still debatable (and said by many to not be the best :/ ).The object itself, imbued with code,created by code or//and binary number can live on for future generations for both humans and computers.Its an immortal object as long as one of those two continue to exist.
